eecOloo

A composting toilet designed around the full festival lifecycle.

Project
Bachelor thesis — Product Development
Year
2024
Role
Product Designer
← PreviousNext →

A festival toilet has to work for far more people than the person using it. Visitors need a clean and intuitive experience, while organisers, cleaners, transporters and waste handlers need a system that is easy to move, install, maintain and process afterwards. The challenge was therefore to design a composting toilet around the entire festival lifecycle rather than treating it as a standalone product.

The concept was developed around six stages, each involving different stakeholders and different requirements. The cabin changes from a compact transport unit into an operational toilet and finally into a system that can be disassembled for cleaning and compost processing. Designing each stage together created one continuous system rather than a collection of separate solutions.

Modularity became the core of the technical solution. A steel frame connects the transport, user and processing functions in one structure. Moving and locking components allow the compact unit to open into a full toilet, while the waste containers and service components remain accessible from the rear for maintenance and processing.

To reduce responsibility for the user, I developed a semi-automatic sawdust dispenser. A single push mechanically releases a measured amount of sawdust into the toilet, helping cover the waste while keeping the interaction simple and independent of electronics.

The modular architecture was tested through physical models. A scale prototype helped evaluate how the cabin transforms between compact and operational states, while a separate working prototype was used to test the mechanical sawdust dispenser.
